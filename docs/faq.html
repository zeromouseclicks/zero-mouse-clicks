Questions.
Straight answers.

Everything people ask about turning a floor plan image into a plan set with Zero Mouse Clicks.

What is Zero Mouse Clicks?

A free, open-source tool that turns the floor plan image from a developer's brochure or a real-estate listing into an architectural plan set: eight A3 drawing sheets in one PDF, with walls, doors, windows, dimensions, room areas, schedules, finishes, a schematic electrical layout, a furniture layout and two sections. It runs in your web browser.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no account, no sign-up and no payment. It can be free because the work happens on your own device: there is no server and no AI service to pay for.

Is my floor plan uploaded anywhere?

No. The image is processed in your browser and never leaves your device. Only if you choose to write to us through the contact form, and attach a file, does that file get sent.

Does it use ChatGPT or another large language model?

No. There are no LLM calls and no tokens. Three small segmentation models, between 0.8 and 1.3 MB each, read the image, and explicit rules draw the plans.

What kind of image works best?

The floor plan image a developer or a listing publishes, in colour or as a 3D top view, at least about 1,000 px wide and taken straight on. Plans that print room dimensions give the best scale. Images under about 800 px lose walls.

Can I photograph a printed brochure with my phone?

Yes, if the photo is sharp and taken straight on. Perspective is not corrected yet, so angled photos often fail. A screenshot or the original image file works better.

How accurate are the dimensions?

Marketing plans are not drawn to scale. When the plan prints room dimensions and they are read, rooms come out within about 1 cm of the printed values and room areas within about 0.6 % (median on the measurement bench). With only a declared area, accuracy depends on how that area was measured. Always verify on site.

Which area should I type?

None, if the plan prints room dimensions or the area. Otherwise type the private (net interior) area in m², or the gross area in sq ft, as US listings usually give it. The tool switches between m² and sq ft.

Does it work with US listings and feet and inches?

Areas in sq ft are supported. Dimensions in feet and inches are rarely read yet, and US listings are largely untested, so typing the area helps.

What exactly do I get?

One A3 PDF with eight sheets: G-001 cover and index, A-101 floor plan, A-102 schedules, A-103 floor finishes, A-104 schematic electrical, A-105 furniture layout, A-201 kitchen section and A-202 bathroom section. See each sheet.

Can I get DWG, DXF or Revit files?

Not from the site yet; DXF export is on the roadmap. For an editable CAD file, write to us.

Are the sections and the electrical plan real?

No. A floor plan does not contain heights or wiring, so sections use assumed heights (2.45 m clear, 0.15 m slab, 2.10 m doors) and the electrical layout is a rule-based proposal. Both are marked as schematic.

Can I use the drawings for a permit or for construction?

They are drawn from a marketing image, not from a measured survey. Verify every dimension on site and have a professional review the drawings before using them for permits, fabrication or construction.

How long does it take?

About a minute on a recent computer; the first run also loads the engine. It runs on Android phones; on iPhone it needs iOS 16.4 or later and may run out of memory with large images.

Can I run it on my own computer or in my software?

Yes. The Python package zmc, with a command line and an API, is on GitHub under the Apache-2.0 licence, which allows commercial use. It needs no GPU.

Who makes it?

The Zero Mouse Clicks contributors. It is an open-source project: the code, the model weights and the training recipe are public.

Why the name?

Drafting a plan by hand in CAD takes hundreds of clicks: walls, doors, dimensions. The idea is to read the image instead of tracing it. See the race.

It didn't work on my plan. What now?

Tell us through the contact form and choose “It didn't work”, or open an issue on GitHub. Attach the plan only if you have the right to share it.
