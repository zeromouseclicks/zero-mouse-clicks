How it works.
Read, then draw.

Zero Mouse Clicks turns the floor plan image from a developer's brochure or a real-estate listing into an architectural plan set: eight A3 drawing sheets in one PDF. Three tiny AI models read the image, explicit rules draw it, and all of it runs in your browser. The image is never uploaded.

1. Drop the plan

Start from the floor plan image a developer or a listing publishes: the colourful one with furniture, wood floors, room names and a few dimensions. A screenshot or a saved JPG or PNG works best. Images narrower than about 800 px lose walls, so use the largest version you can find. You can also photograph a printed brochure, as long as the photo is sharp and taken straight on: perspective is not corrected yet.

Typing the area is optional. If the plan prints room dimensions, the scale comes from them. If it doesn't, type the private (net interior) area in m², or the gross area in sq ft for a US listing.

2. Tiny models read it

Three small U-Net segmentation models, of 190 k to 330 k parameters and 0.8 to 1.3 MB each, label every pixel of the image: background, interior space, wall, door, window, counter or closet, fixed fixture, and loose furniture. The pipeline crops the image to the plan and averages the three models at three resolutions.

The models were trained only on synthetic plans. Code in the repository builds random apartments with rules and draws them in brochure styles (textured floors, furniture, coloured backgrounds, labels, logos), so every pixel comes with its exact label. No real floor plan and no third-party dataset was used for training.

A text reader (OCR) picks up room names in English and Spanish, the declared area and printed room dimensions such as 2.69 x 2.20 or 12'4" x 11'0".

3. Scale from the printed dimensions

Marketing plans are not drawn to scale, so the scale has to be inferred. Every printed dimension is compared with the clear spans measured on the image, face to face of walls, in X and Y separately. The scale that explains most of them within ±3.5 % wins. A wrong declared area cannot spoil it, and a stretched image gets corrected.

Then the dimensions that agree also fix their own room: each wall line moves a few pixels, both faces together, so the clear span matches what is printed. Dimensions that contradict the drawing are reported, not forced. On the measurement bench, rooms come out within about 1 cm of the printed values and room areas within about 0.6 % (median).

Without printed dimensions, the declared area is used with its definition: a private area is about the net spaces plus half of the partitions; a built, gross or sq ft area is about the footprint with walls.

4. Rules draw it

From the masks, explicit rules build a vector model in metres:

  • walls become orthogonal bars, and openings are the gaps between them;
  • room types come from the text read on the plan, or from rules when there is none;
  • counters, closets and fixtures are drawn as standard blocks;
  • bathrooms follow the usual layout: a wall-to-wall shower at one end, toilet and lavatory on one wall;
  • kitchen appliances sit against a wall next to the counters; dining chairs get 0.60 m per seat;
  • each piece of furniture gets a type, a catalogue size and the direction it faces.

Every decision is code you can read and change. That is deliberate: a wrong result can be traced to one rule.

5. One PDF, eight sheets

The vector model is drawn as one A3 PDF with eight sheets: cover, floor plan with dimensions, schedules, floor finishes, schematic electrical, furniture layout, and kitchen and bathroom sections. Each sheet has an ISO 5457 frame, a title block and the largest standard scale that fits. See what each sheet contains.

Why it runs in your browser

The page runs the same Python package as the command line (through Pyodide), the three models (through onnxruntime-web) and the text reader (tesseract.js) on your own device. There is no server, no upload, no account and no AI tokens to pay for, which is why the tool can be free.

A plan takes about a minute on a recent computer; the first run also loads the engine. It runs on Android phones. On iPhone it needs iOS 16.4 or later and may run out of memory with large images, so a computer is the safest choice.

Open source

The code, the model weights and the training recipe are on GitHub under the Apache-2.0 licence, also for commercial use. From Python:

pip install -e .            # inside the cloned repository
zmc my_plan.jpg --area 47 -o plan-set.pdf

Always improving

Zero Mouse Clicks is an AI project, and AI moves fast. The models, the rules and the drawings change often, and every release reads plans better and draws them more precisely. Recent releases fitted walls to the printed dimensions, laid out bathrooms, kitchens and dining tables the way a designer would, and added the furniture layout.

Next on the list: a one-tap correction screen, DXF export, more plan styles and a model that recognises each type of furniture. Follow the changes on GitHub.
